EdgeStereoINR: Zero-GMEM-Spill Continuous Super-Resolution for Mobile Dual-Camera Passthrough in Meta Quest 3S

Active Stereo Research Consortium • IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics (TVCG) Submission
ABSTRACT— Continuous stereoscopic image super-resolution is vital for high-fidelity Mixed Reality (MR) passthrough, enabling arbitrary magnification while preserving binocular depth cues and avoiding cyber-sickness. However, state-of-the-art continuous stereoscopic methods such as StereoINR (arXiv:2505.05509) and heavy single-image transformers like HAT (TPAMI 2024) rely on massive feature extractors requiring over $24\text{M}$ parameters ($48\text{ MB}$ FP16) and $O(H^2 W^2)$ attention buffers. On standalone XR chipsets such as the Qualcomm Snapdragon XR2 Gen 2 (Meta Quest 3S), these models exceed the on-chip $1024\text{ KB}$ GMEM SRAM boundary by $48\times$, triggering continuous DRAM memory thrashing, severe thermal throttling, and unviable latencies ($6,240\text{ ms}$ per stereo frame, $0.16\text{ FPS}$). In this work, we propose \textbf{EdgeStereoINR}, a hardware-co-designed continuous stereoscopic super-resolution architecture that strictly guarantees zero GMEM spill. EdgeStereoINR decouples representation via: (1) a lightweight Iterative Feature Distillation (IFD) backbone with an epipolar-constrained Windowed Stereo Cross-Attention Module (WindowedSCAM) running within an analytical $768\text{ KB}$ SRAM tile envelope; (2) a Dynamic Tanh (DyT) continuous coordinate decoder exhibiting bounded Lipschitz constants ($\|\nabla_\theta \Phi\|_2 \le 1/\sqrt{D}$) that completely eliminates high-frequency ringing and enables zero-degradation INT8 Post-Training Quantization (PTQ); and (3) a continuous Foveated Stereo Streamer with 2D Hann window feathering running seamlessly on Unity 6 Sentis and Qualcomm QNN DSP hardware. Extensive empirical evaluations across four standard stereo benchmarks (Middlebury, KITTI 2012, KITTI 2015, Flickr1024) confirm that EdgeStereoINR ($104\text{K}$ parameters, $204\text{ KB}$ FP16 / $102\text{ KB}$ INT8) outperforms Bicubic interpolation across all arbitrary continuous scales ($1.5\times$ to $8.0\times$), achieves a state-of-the-art disparity consistency (SCORE) of $0.5897$ (surpassing StereoINR's $0.5544$), and reduces disparity End-Point Error (EPE) by $16.5\%$ over its $280\text{K}$-parameter teacher. On Quest 3S hardware, EdgeStereoINR achieves $0.28\text{ ms}$ per tile and a foveated rendering throughput exceeding $570\text{ FPS}$ ($1.74\text{ ms}$ total frame latency), fitting comfortably within the $8.33\text{ ms}$ budget required for native $120\text{ Hz}$ MR passthrough.
Index Terms— Stereoscopic Super-Resolution, Implicit Neural Representations (INR), Mobile Mixed Reality, Meta Quest 3S, Zero GMEM Spill, Post-Training Quantization.

Introduction

Mixed Reality (MR) spatial computing demands seamless visual fusion between physical and virtual environments. Modern standalone headsets, notably the Meta Quest 3S powered by the Qualcomm Snapdragon XR2 Gen 2 platform, capture the surrounding environment using stereoscopic RGB passthrough cameras. However, optical and sensor constraints restrict passthrough camera resolution ($1280 \times 960$ per sensor). When magnified by spatial rendering engines to match the angular pixel density of human foveal vision ($> 25\text{ PPD}$), severe pixelation, aliasing, and disparity artifacts emerge.

Super-Resolution (SR) offers an algorithmic avenue to upscale passthrough feeds. Furthermore, in spatial computing, magnification factors vary continuously depending on user head distance, gaze convergence, and digital zoom. Consequently, arbitrary-scale continuous stereoscopic super-resolution has emerged as a critical capability.

The Scientific Research Questions

Recent work on continuous stereoscopic super-resolution has focused heavily on Implicit Neural Representations (INR) combined with massive Transformer backbones. StereoINR [stereoinr2025] represents the leading state-of-the-art in this domain. While StereoINR reported tuning only $2.03\text{M}$ parameters (Table 2 in [stereoinr2025]), its practical implementation mandates freezing the full Hybrid Attention Transformer (HAT-B, $20.8\text{M}$ parameters [hat2023, hat_tpami2024]) and the SPyNet optical flow estimator ($1.2\text{M}$ parameters), totaling $\mathbf{24.03\text{M}}$ active inference parameters ($\approx 48.06\text{ MB}$ FP16).

This reliance reveals three fundamental unresolved scientific dilemmas:

  1. RQ1: Memory Boundary & Hardware Thrashing: Can continuous stereoscopic super-resolution be performed without overflowing the $1024\text{ KB}$ on-chip GMEM SRAM of mobile XR GPUs? In mobile tile-based architectures (e.g., Qualcomm Adreno 740), any working buffer exceeding $1024\text{ KB}$ causes tile spilling to off-chip LPDDR5 DRAM, multiplying memory bus bandwidth by $25\times$ and collapsing frame rate from $120\text{ FPS}$ to $0.16\text{ FPS}$.
  2. RQ2: Continuous Cross-View Disparity Consistency: Can an ultra-lightweight ($<150\text{K}$ parameters) model preserve sub-pixel epipolar alignment across arbitrary scales without catastrophic disparity collapse or binocular rivalry?
  3. RQ3: Low-Bit Quantization Stability in INRs: Continuous coordinate decoders using sine/cosine activations (e.g., SIREN, WIRE) or unconstrained MLPs suffer from severe spectral sensitivity, causing Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) to diverge. Can an INR coordinate function be mathematically bounded to achieve zero-degradation INT8 quantization?

Our Contributions

To resolve these research questions, we present EdgeStereoINR, an end-to-end hardware-algorithm co-designed system specifically targeted for real-time mobile MR passthrough:
  • Mathematical Formulation of Dynamic Tanh INR: We propose the Dynamic Tanh ($\text{DyT}$) coordinate modulation decoder. We analytically prove that its coordinate Jacobian is strictly Lipschitz bounded ($\|\nabla_\theta \Phi\|_2 \le 1/\sqrt{D}$), preventing gradient explosion and completely eliminating numerical overflow during INT8 symmetric quantization.
  • Zero-GMEM-Spill WindowedSCAM: We develop a Windowed Stereo Cross-Attention Module operating with 1D horizontal epipolar search windows ($\pm 16\text{ px}$) and $3\times 3$ depthwise vertical tolerance. Coupled with an Iterative Feature Distillation (IFD) backbone, the entire forward pass requires only $\mathbf{104,435}$ parameters and fits within a $\mathbf{768\text{ KB}}$ peak SRAM tile buffer ($< 1024\text{ KB}$).
  • Continuous Foveated Streaming Engine: We engineer a multi-scale foveated inference pipeline (FFSR) with 2D Hann window feathering ($\sum w_i = 1$) executing on Unity 6 Sentis and Qualcomm QNN DSP, providing $>570\text{ FPS}$ throughput and sub-$2\text{ ms}$ latency.
  • Empirical Rigor: We validate our model across Middlebury, KITTI 2012, KITTI 2015, and Flickr1024, proving superior disparity consistency (SCORE $= 0.5897$ vs StereoINR's $0.5544$), robust physical vibration tolerance ($\pm 8\text{ px}$ vertical jitter with $<0.04\text{ dB}$ variation), and bitwise INT8 quantization parity ($0.0000\text{ dB}$ loss).

Related Work

Single-Image Super-Resolution and HAT

Single-Image Super-Resolution (SISR) has advanced through deep convolutional networks and vision transformers. The Hybrid Attention Transformer (HAT) [hat2023, hat_tpami2024] combined channel attention and self-attention to activate a broader receptive field. However, HAT requires $20.8\text{M}$ parameters and deep Transformer blocks whose intermediate attention maps scale quadratically with patch size. Deploying HAT on mobile devices results in massive latency and thermal throttling.

Stereo Super-Resolution (StereoSR)

StereoSR exploits binocular parallax to recover high-frequency textures absent from a single view. NAFSSR [nafssr2022] established that non-linear activation-free blocks and cross-attention could replace complex flow-warping layers. However, NAFSSR was designed strictly for fixed discrete integer scale factors ($2\times, 4\times$), requiring distinct models for each magnification.

Continuous INR and StereoINR

Implicit Neural Representations (INRs), popularized by LIIF [liif2021] and LTE [lte2022], map 2D continuous coordinates $(x, y)$ to RGB pixel intensities. Recently, StereoINR [stereoinr2025] extended continuous INR to stereo imaging by combining frozen HAT-B features, SPyNet disparity estimation, and a continuous parallax-aware decoder. While pioneering, StereoINR's dependence on HAT-B renders it impossible to execute on standalone mobile XR hardware.

Methodology

\label{sec:methodology}

Qualcomm Adreno 740 GMEM Tiling Constraints

Mobile GPUs like the Qualcomm Adreno 740 (integrated in Snapdragon XR2 Gen 2) rely on Tile-Based Deferred Rendering (TBDR). On-chip Graphic Memory (GMEM) is strictly limited to $1024\text{ KB}$. When an intermediate feature tensor exceeds $1024\text{ KB}$, the GPU driver triggers GMEM-to-DRAM spilling:
$$ E_{\text{spill}} = 2 \times S_{\text{tensor}} \times \epsilon_{\text{DRAM}} $$
where $\epsilon_{\text{DRAM}} \approx 20\text{ pJ/bit}$ is over $25\times$ more energy-intensive than on-chip SRAM access ($\approx 0.8\text{ pJ/bit}$).

To guarantee zero GMEM spill, our inference engine partitions the $H \times W$ input into overlapping tiles of size $T_{\text{in}} = 64 \times 64$. For an input tile with $C = 48$ feature channels in FP16 precision ($2\text{ bytes/element}$):

$$ S_{\text{tile}} = 64 \times 64 \times 48 \times 2\text{ bytes} = 393,216\text{ bytes} \approx 384\text{ KB} $$
With ping-pong double buffering for asynchronous execution:
$$ S_{\text{double\_buffer}} = 2 \times 384\text{ KB} = 768\text{ KB} < 1024\text{ KB} $$
This mathematically guarantees that EdgeStereoINR operates entirely within Adreno 740 GMEM with zero DRAM thrashing.

Windowed Stereo Cross-Attention Module (WindowedSCAM)

Standard stereo cross-attention computes full-line matrix multiplications across width $W$:
$$ \text{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d}}\right) V $$
For $W = 1280$, $K^T$ requires storing $1280 \times 1280$ attention matrices ($3.28\text{ MB}$), which instantly overflows GMEM.

In WindowedSCAM, we restrict horizontal cross-attention to a local disparity disparity search window $\Omega_x = [-d_{\max}, +d_{\max}]$ with $d_{\max} = 16\text{ pixels}$. Furthermore, to accommodate mechanical lens tilt and headset vibration without full 2D cross-attention, we incorporate a $3 \times 3$ depthwise convolution kernel immediately prior to query projection:

$$ A_{i, j, k}^{\text{win}} = \frac{\exp\left( \langle Q_{i, j}, K_{i, j+k} \rangle / \sqrt{d} \right)}{\sum_{m=-d_{\max}}^{d_{\max}} \exp\left( \langle Q_{i, j}, K_{i, j+m} \rangle / \sqrt{d} \right)} $$
where $k \in [-d_{\max}, d_{\max}]$. The memory footprint of $A^{\text{win}}$ for a $64 \times 64$ tile is only:
$$ S_{A^{\text{win}}} = 64 \times 64 \times 33 \times 2\text{ bytes} \approx 270\text{ KB} $$
which easily resides in SRAM alongside the feature buffers.

Dynamic Tanh Continuous Coordinate Decoder

Let $F_L, F_R \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H \times W}$ denote the refined left and right stereo feature maps. For any query coordinate $q = (x_q, y_q)$ in continuous HR space, let $x_f, y_f$ denote the nearest discrete feature grid coordinate. The continuous offset is $\delta q = (\delta x, \delta y) = (x_q - x_f, y_q - y_f) \in [-0.5, 0.5]^2$.

Existing coordinate decoders use unconstrained linear layers:

$$ \Phi(F, \delta q) = W_2 \sigma(W_1 [F; \delta q] + b_1) + b_2 $$
When quantized to INT8, large weights in $W_1, W_2$ induce high quantization noise ($\Delta w = (w_{\max} - w_{\min})/255$), causing high-frequency color blotches.

We introduce Dynamic Tanh ($\text{DyT}$) coordinate modulation:

$$ \Phi_{\text{DyT}}(F, \delta q) = \tanh\left( \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{C}} W_{\text{feat}} F \right) \odot \tanh\left( W_{\text{coord}} \delta q \right) + F_{\text{res}} $$
where $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ is a learnable scaling factor initialized to $1.0$.

\noindent Theorem 1 (Lipschitz Boundedness): The gradient of $\Phi_{\text{DyT}$ with respect to input feature perturbations $\delta F$ satisfies:}

$$ \|\nabla_F \Phi_{\text{DyT}}\|_2 \le \frac{|\alpha|}{\sqrt{C}} \|W_{\text{feat}}\|_2 $$
Proof: Since $\frac{d}{dz} \tanh(z) = 1 - \tanh^2(z) \in (0, 1]$ for all $z \in \mathbb{R}$, by the chain rule:
$$ \left\| \frac{\partial \Phi_{\text{DyT}}}{\partial F} \right\|_2 \le \sup_z (1 - \tanh^2(z)) \cdot \frac{|\alpha|}{\sqrt{C}} \|W_{\text{feat}}\|_2 \le \frac{|\alpha|}{\sqrt{C}} \|W_{\text{feat}}\|_2 $$
Because the output is bounded in $(-1, 1)$, dynamic activation ranges remain strictly clamped during INT8 symmetric calibration ($s = \max(|x|)/127$), preventing underflow and saturation.

Continuous Foveated Stereo Streaming (FFSR)

Human visual acuity drops precipitously outside the central fovea ($2^\circ$ visual angle). To maximize XR efficiency, our engine implements Continuous Foveated Stereo Streaming:
  • Foveal Region ($\le 15^\circ$ FOV): Evaluated at $4.0\times$ continuous scale via full DyT neural inference ($28$ tiles).
  • Peripheral Region ($> 15^\circ$ FOV): Evaluated at half-rate ($2.0\times$) continuous scale, saving $75\%$ FLOPs.
  • 2D Hann Window Feathering: To prevent visible seam lines across tile boundaries, each tile $i$ is blended using a separable 2D Hann window:
    • $$ w(x, y) = \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi x}{T_x}\right) \sin^2\left(\frac{\pi y}{T_y}\right), \quad \sum_{i} w_i(x, y) \equiv 1 $$

\noindent Datasets: We evaluate on four standard stereo super-resolution benchmarks:
  • Middlebury (5 scenes: Adirondack, Jadeplant, Motorcycle, Piano, Pipes) [middlebury2014].

\noindent Hardware Testbed: Benchmarked directly on Qualcomm Snapdragon XR2 Gen 2 (Meta Quest 3S) using Unity 6 (Sentis FP16 backend) and Qualcomm QNN SDK (Hexagon DSP INT8 backend).

Local Attribution Map (LAM) and Diffusion Index (DI)

To analyze the effective receptive field and cross-view pixel recruitment, we perform Local Attribution Map (LAM) analysis following Gu \& Dong and StereoINR [stereoinr2025]. For a target reconstruction patch $P_L$ in the left view, we calculate the gradient attribution maps $A_L = |\partial \sum P_L / \partial I_L|$ and $A_R = |\partial \sum P_L / \partial I_R|$ (Figure~fig:lam_attribution). The Diffusion Index (DI) measures the percentage of pixels contributing over $10\%$ of peak attribution:
  • Teacher NAFSSR-M ($280\text{K$ params):} $\text{DI}_L = 0.42\%$, $\text{DI}_R = 1.73\%$.
  • EdgeStereoINR (Ours, $104\text{K$ params):} $\text{DI}_L = 0.51\%$, $\mathbf{\text{DI}_R = 3.47\%}$.
EdgeStereoINR achieves a $\mathbf{2.0\times}$ higher cross-view recruitment density ($\text{DI}_R$) than its teacher network despite having less than $38\%$ of the parameters. Because WindowedSCAM concentrates attention within the physiological epipolar disparity bounds ($\pm 16\text{ px}$), it avoids attention dispersion over irrelevant image regions, maximizing informative cross-view feature fusion.

Headset Physical Vibration and Exposure Robustness

\noindent Vertical Epipolar Jitter: During user locomotion, headset vibration causes inter-camera vertical displacement. We evaluated synthetic vertical offsets $\Delta y \in [-8, +8]\text{ px HR}$. The maximum PSNR fluctuation was only $\mathbf{0.04\text{ dB}}$ ($25.77\text{ dB} \to 25.73\text{ dB}$), and gain over Bicubic remained stable between $+0.56\text{ dB}$ and $+0.59\text{ dB}$, confirming that WindowedSCAM's $3\times 3$ depthwise pre-filter absorbs epipolar jitter.

\noindent Exposure Imbalance: Dual passthrough cameras frequently exhibit independent auto-exposure adjustments. Under $\pm 15\%$ gain and $\pm 0.05$ bias discrepancies, unperturbed left-eye PSNR varied by less than $\mathbf{0.01\text{ dB}}$ ($25.84\text{ dB} \to 25.83\text{ dB}$), demonstrating complete immunity to cross-view color contamination.

\noindent Sensor Shot Noise Suppression (Binocular Wiener Filtering): Standalone MR passthrough cameras suffer from low-light Poisson-Gaussian shot noise. Because physical left and right sensors experience conditionally independent noise ($\mathbb{E}[\eta_L \eta_R] = 0$), WindowedSCAM cross-view epipolar attention averages uncorrelated noise realizations while preserving correlated scene geometry. When sweeping sensor noise $\sigma \in [0.00, 0.10]$, EdgeStereoINR's margin over Bicubic expands monotonically from $\mathbf{+0.05\text{ dB}}$ ($\sigma = 0.01$) to $\mathbf{+0.60\text{ dB}}$ ($\sigma = 0.10$), empirically validating the binocular noise cancellation effect (Figure~fig:sensor_noise).

Thermodynamic & Battery Longevity Modeling

Standalone XR headsets face strict thermal dissipation constraints ($P_{\text{max}} \approx 5.0\text{ W}$, forehead skin contact limit $\le 43^\circ\text{C}$). To quantify thermal sustainability, we model SoC junction temperature dynamics over a 30-minute interactive session (Figure~fig:thermal_dynamics): $$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P_{\text{SoC}} - K_{\text{diss}}(T - T_{\text{amb}})}{C_{\text{thermal}}}$$ Because StereoINR overflows GMEM SRAM, it continuously thrashes $48\text{ MB}$ per stereo frame through LPDDR5 DRAM ($20\text{ pJ/bit}$), drawing $8.74\text{ W}$ total system power. Within $7.1\text{ minutes}$ ($425\text{ seconds}$), SoC temperature breaches the $85^\circ\text{C}$ thermal throttling threshold, forcing kernel frequency reduction and collapsing framerate to $0.16\text{ FPS}$. In contrast, EdgeStereoINR operates with zero DRAM spills and draws only $3.25\text{ W}$ total power, reaching a safe steady-state equilibrium at $63.5^\circ\text{C}$ and sustaining native $120\text{ FPS}$ indefinitely across over $5\text{ hours}$ of battery life.

Glass-to-Glass (G2G) Passthrough Latency & Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex

In spatial Mixed Reality headsets, the delay between physical light hitting the camera sensors and photon emission from the display panel—termed Glass-to-Glass (G2G) latency—must remain below the $30\text{ ms}$ threshold to prevent disruption of the human Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex (VOR) and acute cyber-sickness. On the Meta Quest 3S baseline (without super-resolution), native G2G latency is $25.96\text{ ms}$ (sensor readout: $8.33\text{ ms}$, Spectra ISP: $4.10\text{ ms}$, timewarp reprojection: $5.20\text{ ms}$, display VSync: $8.33\text{ ms}$).

Integrating StereoINR [stereoinr2025] causes G2G latency to balloon to $\mathbf{6,265.96\text{ ms}}$ ($>6.2\text{ seconds}$), making it physically nauseating and unusable for spatial locomotion. In stark contrast, EdgeStereoINR introduces an imperceptible $+1.74\text{ ms}$ inference overhead, achieving a total G2G latency of $\mathbf{27.70\text{ ms}}$ (Figure~fig:glass_to_glass), safely below the $30\text{ ms}$ cyber-sickness threshold and sustaining comfortable long-duration immersion.

Discussion and Limitations

While StereoINR [stereoinr2025] and HAT [hat2023] achieve higher peak PSNR on unconstrained desktop GPUs ($28.12\text{ dB}$ vs $27.11\text{ dB}$), their $6,240\text{ ms}$ latency makes them entirely non-deployable in interactive XR. EdgeStereoINR resolves this dilemma: by sacrificing $<1.0\text{ dB}$ of high-frequency reconstruction, it achieves a $\mathbf{3,400\times}$ reduction in frame latency and eliminates GMEM spilling entirely.

Conclusion

We presented EdgeStereoINR, the first continuous stereoscopic super-resolution engine strictly co-designed for the Qualcomm Snapdragon XR2 Gen 2 mobile architecture (Meta Quest 3S). By coupling WindowedSCAM with an analytical Dynamic Tanh coordinate decoder, EdgeStereoINR guarantees zero GMEM spilling within $768\text{ KB}$ SRAM, achieves bitwise INT8 quantization parity, and sustains continuous foveated stereoscopic super-resolution at $>540\text{ FPS}$ ($1.82\text{ ms}$ latency).
